Benchmark v1

Real agents, an open model, old hardware. Every number labelled.

These are measured results with the hardware, model, image and method on the page. They are single runs, so they are evidence and not certified bars. Certified results come with our paper.

What was measured, and how much to trust it.

Two things were measured. First, real agents calling a real open-weight model through the one outbound checkpoint. Second, how many calls a single VM can hold open at once, for five VM sizes.

Each result below is a single run. Our own rule is 3 runs, report the median, and treat more than 10% spread between runs as a failure. Single runs do not meet that rule, so they are labelled as evidence. The same harness will run three times on the final image, and those certified numbers will be published with our paper.

The memory figures are the runtime's total runtime memory as the harness reported it, not operating-system RSS.

Real agents, one open model, one checkpoint.

Real open model · 2 vCPU / 4 GB only

Every model call goes through the single outbound checkpoint. Two separate runs are shown here, and the numbers are not added together. Real-model runs on every VM size, 3 runs each, are in progress for the paper.

How many calls one VM can hold open.

The model is replaced by a 5 ms stand-in, so the test measures the runtime alone. Five VM sizes, four load levels, 20 runs. 0 refused in all 20 runs. 150,000 calls in flight fit on 1 vCPU and 1 GB at 212 MB. More processors did not help, because the work is wait-bound: each call spends its time waiting.

Capacity figures use a 5 ms mock model, so they measure VOLENT, not a model. Certified three-run results on the final image will follow with our paper.

Method.

Everything needed to judge these numbers, and to check them against the raw files.

  • Hardware. One Intel i7-4770 from 2013, 8 cores, 31 GB, running guests under KVM. Capacity guests came in five shapes: 1 vCPU with 1 GB, 2 with 2 GB, 2 with 4 GB, 4 with 8 GB and 8 with 16 GB. The real-model runs used 2 vCPU with 4 GB.
  • Image. A pre-release build, digest cf922365, taken before the final phase closed. It is not the frozen release image.
  • Model. llama3.2:1b through Ollama on the host, CPU inference, over TLS and HTTP/2, reached through the checkpoint. The capacity runs use a stand-in model with a fixed 5 ms delay.
  • Corpus. 24 tasks in four tiers: recall, arithmetic, reasoning and structured output. Corpus digest 9d19af46e999. 32 runs per task, 8 at a time.
  • Rule. Our standard is 3 runs, median reported, and more than 10% spread counts as a failure. These are single runs and say so.
  • Raw data. The raw files are archived byte for byte, with sha256 checksums for each. The archive tarball's sha256 starts d9bb2569. Every cell in the tables above is checked against those files before this page is published.
  • To rerun. Boot the image on a machine like this one, point it at a local open-weight model, run the 24-task corpus 32 times at 8 at once, then run the five VM sizes at the four load levels. The same harness will run three times on the final image.

How to compare

Snapshot and warm-pool products quote start-up times on modern servers. This is a cold start of a whole sealed system on 2013 hardware. We do not quote any competitor's numbers here.
